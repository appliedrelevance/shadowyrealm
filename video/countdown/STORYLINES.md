# The countdown: three storylines

The segment: No. 10 to No. 1 on the finished river, each entry's band lit with a card, as a
vertical YouTube Short that loops: 1080 x 1920, 30 fps, 120 s, our own score, no voice. One build
serves all five rankings (Top 10 of all time, Best, Worst, Purest Good, Purest Evil): the ranking is
a key in `FILMS`. The storylines are told here for Worst, ranked by benefit minus harm. Every card
is held for its words: the Worst ten need 92.3 s of reading out of the 111 s after the hook.

Worst, from the snapshot of 2026-11-17 (the live scores match it on 2026-11-28): 1 First
Jewish–Roman War (66–73), 2 Atlantic slave trade (1501–1867), 3 Neo-Assyrian Empire (911–609 BCE),
4 Spanish Empire (1492–1898), 5 Spanish conquest of the Inca Empire (1532–1572), 6 Three Kingdoms
(220–280), 7 Age of Discovery (1415–1600), 8 Black Death (1346–1353), 9 Viking Age (793–1066),
10 Columbian exchange (1492–1700).

## A. Lights out (recommended)

**Device:** a number lands, ten times. **Form:** one frame that rebuilds.

The finished river holds the screen for the whole film. For each entry the river goes dark except
that entry's band, which lights in its own benefit and harm where it lived, while a huge number and
its card land above it; when the next number comes, the last band stays lit at half strength, so by
No. 1 the ten burn together and show where in time the worst things sit.

- **First two seconds:** the river dark but for ten lit bands; "THE 10 WORST / THINGS IN HISTORY"
  at the top; "#1" and the First Jewish–Roman War's card over its band at 66 CE, all on screen at
  frame 0.
- **Beats:** hook (No. 1 and the title) → lights out (the ten go dark) → No. 10 to No. 2, one band
  lit each, the lit ones adding up → No. 1 → the title returns, and the last frame is frame 0.
- **Look:** the films' ground `#181822`, Inter, benefit `#3e9ad6`, harm `#d9534f`. The material is
  light: a lit band at full colour against the rest of the river at 12%.
- **Sound:** our own score in SoundCraft on the race's themes in D minor; a low stroke (timpani and
  basses) on each number, each a step heavier, the crest on No. 1, soft at frame 0 so the join is
  quiet.

## B. Time machine

**Device:** the year is the ride. **Form:** a flight through one world.

The camera rides the river through time to each entry: it zooms until the entry's lifespan fills
the width, and the year counter spins forwards or back as it travels, so every number is a jump
through history the viewer feels (the last flight runs from the Atlantic slave trade's 1501 back to
66 CE). At each stop the band rises to full height and the card lands; after No. 1 the camera pulls
back to the whole river.

- **First two seconds:** the river zoomed to 66–73 CE, the war's red band filling the width, the
  year "66 CE" huge at the top, "#1" and the title over it.
- **Beats:** hook (No. 1 in close-up and the title) → the pull-back to five thousand years → the
  flight to No. 10's era → nine more flights and stops → No. 1 in close-up, and the last frame is
  frame 0.
- **Look:** as A. The material is the year counter, huge, the speedometer of the ride.
- **Sound:** as A, with a string rise under each flight that lands on the stroke as the card
  arrives.

## C. Cut out

**Device:** the river gives them up. **Form:** object-to-object chain.

Each entry's band is cut out of the river and carried up to a board at the top, laid there as a
ribbon at its own length with its picture, No. 10 at the bottom and each number above the last,
while the river closes over the gap it left. By No. 1 the board holds the ten and the river below
is visibly thinner where they lived.

- **First two seconds:** the full board of ten ribbons at the top, No. 1 leading it, the thinned
  river below, the title over the board.
- **Beats:** hook (the full board and the title) → the ten fly home into the river → No. 10 cut out
  and carried up → nine more → No. 1 on top, and the last frame is frame 0.
- **Look:** as A. The material is the band as a cut ribbon that keeps its curve as its shape.
- **Sound:** as A, with a harp and pizzicato pluck as each band comes free and the stroke as it
  lands on the board.
